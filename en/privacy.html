Privacy Policy

This app doesn't touch your data

On Person watches one Bluetooth link: the one between your Apple Watch and your iPhone. It never goes online, never collects personal information, and never asks for location. Below is exactly what it leaves on your device.

Effective 2026-11-12　·　Applies to version 1.0　·　iOS and watchOS

Privacy Policy Support

1 · What we explicitly do not do

  • No personal information is collected. No account, no email, no phone number, no device identifier, no advertising identifier (IDFA).
  • Nothing is uploaded. Nothing goes over the network. There is no network request anywhere in the app — not a promise to refrain, the code simply isn't there.
  • Your location is never read. No location permission, no coordinate conversion, no location history.
  • No third-party SDKs. No analytics, no crash reporting, no ad SDK, no backend server.
The one exception is the App Store purchase and restore. That step is handled entirely by Apple's own system framework (StoreKit). We never see your Apple ID, your payment details, or your bill, and we don't read them.

2 · What the app keeps on your device

Everything lives in the local App Group container (group.com.nearby.antiloss), shared between the phone and the watch. Three kinds of content, all together well under a few dozen kilobytes.

What an event record actually contains

Four things and nothing more: a random ID, the moment it happened, the kind of event (nine in total — signal weak entering grace, grace ended and alert fired, recorded during a quiet period, auto-reconnect succeeded, you came back to your phone, keep guarding, started guarding, stopped guarding, make my phone ring), and one short note (for example “watch is charging”).

What is not in there: no coordinates, no history of where you were, no time series of signal strength, no list of your devices, no advertising identifier, no record of which apps you opened.

3 · System permissions: we ask for three things

Bluetooth (Always)

The iPhone and the Apple Watch each scan for the other, and that Bluetooth link is how the app tells whether you're still together. The system's own wording is: “Used to let your watch notice when your phone is no longer in the same room as you.” Without this permission the app cannot work.

Local Network (iPhone side)

The iPhone and the Apple Watch talk to each other over a direct local link, and the system asks once for “Allow … to use wireless data?”. Our usage string is: “Used to sync guard status and settings between your iPhone and Apple Watch; device-to-device traffic stays on the local link and never goes through a server.” Without it, status and settings cannot travel between phone and watch.

⚠️ Measured on iOS 27: the system dialog shows the system's own wording; our usage string does not appear in it. This is written the way the system actually behaves rather than pretending we can change it.

Notifications (watch side only)

A vibration and a notification on your wrist when you separate. You can choose to let it break through Focus modes (Time Sensitive) — but the app only uses that if you turn it on yourself in Settings; it is off by default. The system does not expose whether Focus is on, so we cannot promise the alert always gets through.

What we never ask for

  • Location (Always / When In Use) — never requested
  • Camera, microphone, photos, contacts — never requested
  • Tracking (App Tracking Transparency / IDFA) — never requested, never used
  • Health, speech recognition, keyboard — never requested

4 · Signal strength is not a location

The signal strength the app reads (RSSI, in dBm) is a number from a radio, not a coordinate. It answers “is that end still answering?” It cannot answer “which end is that?”

So the app does not convert anything into a position, does not request location permission, and keeps no location history. The location tracking common in this category simply isn't here — that feature isn't in the app at all.

5 · Data security, retention and deletion

  • On your device. Everything is written into the App Group container. Nothing goes to iCloud, nothing goes to CloudKit, nothing reaches a server.
  • Not encrypted. That is a deliberate trade: plain text in exchange for “you can open it and check any time.” The cost is no extra layer of encryption — stated here rather than glossed over.
  • Deletion is simple. Clear the log any time from inside the app, or uninstall it — the container goes with it, and nothing is left anywhere another app could read.

6 · Children's privacy

On Person is not designed for children under 13, and it does not knowingly collect personal information from children. Because it collects no personal information at all, there is no exception to make here.
